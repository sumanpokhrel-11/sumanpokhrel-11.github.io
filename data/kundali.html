What Is a Kundali?

A kundali, also called a janma kundali or birth chart, is a map of where the Sun, Moon, and other planets sat in the sky at the exact moment and place of your birth, read against the sidereal (fixed-star) zodiac used in Vedic astrology. It's the foundation that a jyotish uses to discuss personality, timing of life events, and compatibility.

This tool builds your kundali using the same core method professional Vedic astrology software uses: it calculates real planetary positions astronomically, applies the Lahiri ayanamsa to convert them into the sidereal zodiac, and places them into a whole sign house chart anchored on your ascendant. The result is shown as a North Indian style diamond chart, alongside your Navamsa (D9) chart and your Vimshottari Dasha, the traditional 120-year planetary period timeline used for timing predictions.

Nothing here is guesswork. Every planetary longitude, house, and nakshatra placement follows the same classical Parashari method described in the Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra, computed with modern astronomical precision instead of printed tables.

Kundali Terms Reference

TermMeaning
LagnaThe ascendant, the zodiac sign rising on the eastern horizon at birth. Becomes House 1.
RashiYour Moon sign, the zodiac sign the Moon occupied at your birth.
NakshatraOne of 27 lunar mansions the Moon can occupy, each spanning 13°20' of the zodiac.
PadaA quarter subdivision of a nakshatra, 1 to 4, used for finer timing and matching analysis.
Navamsa (D9)The ninth divisional chart, used mainly for marriage and a planet's inner strength.
Vimshottari DashaA 120-year cycle of nine planetary periods used to time events across a lifetime.
AyanamsaThe angular gap between the tropical and sidereal zodiacs, roughly 24° today.

Frequently Asked Questions

This tool uses the Lahiri (Chitrapaksha) ayanamsa, the official standard adopted by the Government of India and used in most Nepali panchang and kundali software. It is recalculated for your exact birth moment rather than using a fixed yearly value.

The planetary positions are calculated using the same class of astronomical model used by professional Vedic astrology software, validated to within about half an arcminute of Swiss Ephemeris output. Accuracy ultimately depends on how precise your entered birth time and place are.

Whole sign houses, the standard used in classical Vedic astrology, where the ascendant's sign becomes the first house and each subsequent sign becomes the next house in order.

The ascendant and house placements shift with birth time, sometimes noticeably even with a few minutes' difference, while planetary signs and nakshatras stay comparatively stable. Use your most accurate recorded birth time for a reliable ascendant.

The Navamsa is the ninth divisional chart, formed by dividing each sign into nine parts of 3 degrees 20 minutes. It is read alongside the main birth chart, most commonly for marriage and inner strength analysis.

This tool uses the true node, matching the convention used by most modern Vedic astrology software including Hamro Patro and Jagannatha Hora, rather than the mean node used in some older classical texts.
